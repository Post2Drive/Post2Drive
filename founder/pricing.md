# Pricing strategy — provisional hypothesis

## Current model
The application README states a one-off 7-day access period with no automatic recurring billing and no per-job fee. UK individual: £9.99; fleet 2–5 vehicles: £16.99; 6–15: £24.99; 16–30: £36.99; 31–55: £49.99; 56+ custom. Supported euro markets use the same numeric tiers in EUR. The source reviewed does not define a validated India price.

## Competitive context
Published alternatives vary substantially: SDCN advertises £39/month + VAT; HaulageBoard currently displays £39.98/month after a promotion plus a fee per agreed load; Pay A Van says it is free to join with commission on completed jobs. These models are not directly equivalent to a 7-day pass, so compare total expected cost for a realistic month and number of completed loads, not headline price alone. Check live terms before launch.

## Recommendation
Do not change the current price based only on desk research. Keep £9.99/7 days as a **test hypothesis**, not an established optimal price. The core risk is perceived value before liquidity is proven.

## Experiments
1. Interview 15–20 active drivers: what they currently pay, loads won per month, and how they decide a new board is worth paying for.
2. Show the same truthful local supply snapshot to matched groups and test two offers: (A) £9.99 for 7 days; (B) free browse with payment only when the driver deliberately activates paid access after seeing the available supply. This is a product/offer experiment, not a promise to change the implemented model without approval.
3. Track activation, paid conversion, first bid, first accepted job, repeat use, refund/support requests and 7-day retention.
4. Separately test India pricing only after choosing a region and collecting local driver interviews.

## Missing evidence
No real customer price-survey data, panel answers, CAC, retention, payment costs, refund rate, support cost, or revenue figures were provided. Therefore no Van Westendorp price range, margin or optimal price is claimed.
