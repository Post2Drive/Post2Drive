# Founder board — Post2Drive

**Status: provisional strategic review; not an investment recommendation.** The votes below are analytical judgments based on the available product brief, not real people or external investors.

## Summary vote

**2 × FUND IF, 1 × PASS (for broad launch now).** The product concept is plausible, but the marketplace should not scale acquisition or claim validated demand until one narrow geography/vehicle segment demonstrates repeat work and driver willingness to pay.

## Offers lens — 6/10 — FUND IF

### What is strong
- The customer outcome is concrete: find suitable paid jobs and reduce unproductive capacity.
- A no-per-job-fee subscription is easy to explain and may be attractive if drivers see enough relevant jobs.
- A unified workflow can reduce friction from job discovery through delivery completion.

### What could kill it
1. Drivers pay but see too few relevant jobs in their routes/vehicle class.
2. The supply of jobs is not dense enough in a launch geography, creating a poor first experience.
3. The platform cannot show credible evidence that the paid access produces value.

### Conditions
- Define one beachhead segment and geography.
- Interview real drivers and load posters; record current tools, prices, job frequency and switching barriers.
- Set a measurable density threshold before charging a broad audience.
- Keep the product's live payment flow disabled until provider-backed settlement and failure paths are verified.

## Monopoly lens — 5/10 — FUND IF

### What is strong
- A narrow corridor or city/vehicle niche could provide a tractable starting market.
- Repeat usage and local network density could become advantages if the platform delivers reliably.

### What could kill it
1. Incumbent load boards already have materially greater job volume and trust.
2. A generic multi-country launch spreads acquisition effort too thinly.
3. No proprietary supply, distribution partnership or trust advantage is evidenced yet.

### Conditions
- Start with one UK niche and one geography/corridor, not a simultaneous broad launch across all markets.
- Interview posters first to understand why they would list jobs on a new board.
- Identify a repeatable channel to acquire the first 20–50 posters and the matching driver cohort.
- Do not claim network effects before observing repeat transactions and retention.

## Product lens — 7/10 — FUND IF

### What is strong
- The core workflow is understandable: post, discover, bid, accept, fulfil.
- Country/currency isolation is an explicit product principle.
- A transparent weekly access model avoids automatic recurring billing in the current design.

### What could kill it
1. Onboarding and verification are not completed end to end.
2. Drivers cannot tell whether a job is credible, suitable, fairly priced or worth the empty miles.
3. The marketplace's broad scope obscures the one thing that must work brilliantly at launch.

### Conditions
- Make the first session show relevant, real and verifiable opportunities—or clearly explain the local launch status.
- Prioritise trust: poster identity, driver eligibility, job details, payment terms, cancellation/dispute handling and proof of delivery.
- Test browser and mobile journeys with real users before public launch.

## Risks raised across lenses
- Marketplace liquidity and job quality are unproven.
- Competitive alternatives already exist in both markets.
- The payment and email provider flows are not verified end to end.
- Customer acquisition, retention, gross margin and support cost are unknown.

## Strongest version of the business
Post2Drive begins as a focused, trustworthy job-discovery and fulfilment marketplace for a defined group of owner-drivers and repeat load posters in one UK geography or corridor. It proves that posters return and drivers find enough suitable paid work to justify access before expanding to other UK regions or launching a separate India-specific growth plan.

## Next step
Run structured interviews with 15–20 UK drivers and 8–10 repeat load posters in one chosen geography. Use the competitor and offer work to test the actual pain and the current £9.99 weekly access hypothesis; do not treat simulated feedback as demand evidence.
