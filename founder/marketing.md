# Marketing plan — first 30 days (validation before scale)

## Positioning hypothesis
“Find delivery jobs that fit your vehicle and route—with clear access pricing and no per-job platform fee.” This must be qualified by real local availability; never promise earnings or job volume without evidence.

## Audience and beachhead
Start with one selected UK region/corridor and one vehicle segment (for example, owner-driver vans). The exact region and segment remain undecided; choose them from interviews and available real job supply rather than guessing. Acquire load posters before driving broad driver acquisition.

## Channels to test
1. Founder-led outreach to local courier firms, freight brokers, small shippers and repeat load posters.
2. Direct outreach to owner-drivers and small fleets in relevant professional groups, with group rules respected.
3. Referral invitations from pilot users only after the product delivers a successful first experience.
4. Landing page with an honest local listing snapshot and a waitlist/pilot sign-up.
5. Local trade/community partnerships after the pilot has a clear value proposition.

## 30-day sequence
- Days 1–7: 15–20 driver interviews and 8–10 poster interviews; document existing channels, job frequency, routes, price sensitivity and trust concerns.
- Days 8–14: recruit a small poster cohort; publish only genuine listings; confirm the operational process for stale listings, cancellations and disputes.
- Days 15–21: invite a matched driver cohort for a controlled pilot; observe first search, first bid and first completed job.
- Days 22–30: interview activated and inactive users; assess whether posters return, drivers find suitable work and the offer is worth paying for. Decide whether to iterate, narrow further or stop.

## Metrics
Track by market and cohort: active posters, valid jobs posted, time to first relevant listing, listing freshness, relevant jobs viewed per driver, bids per job, poster response rate, accepted/completed jobs, repeat posters, repeat drivers, paid conversion, refunds, support burden and acquisition cost. Do not combine UK and India figures.

## Content hooks to test
- “Before you pay for a load board, check how many jobs fit your vehicle and route.”
- “The cheapest platform is not the one with the lowest fee—it is the one that produces work worth taking.” (Use as an opinion, not a quantified claim.)
- “Know the job details and payment terms before you commit.”
- “A new marketplace must earn driver trust before it earns a subscription.”

## Paid ads
Do not scale paid acquisition before there is evidence of local liquidity and repeat usage. No CAC ceiling is set because price, conversion, retention and contribution margin are unknown.
